C3.1 Brooks: Helix and Aider

Method. Tick = 1 month. Veterans = active developers with tenure of at least 6 months; New = developers whose first commit is under 6 months old. The standard model's own production equation is fitted to per-veteran commits: y = p·exp(−(c·V(V−1)/2 + train·New)) (bounded least squares, 300 bootstraps over months), so the evidence and the model use the same formula. Newcomer ramp-up 1 − exp(−r(k+1)) gives mature_rate.

C3.2 aiwork: Aider, two AI eras

Method (GitClear churn construct). Only commits by the same author (Paul Gauthier), within the same period, to aider/*.py. A commit is AI if tagged "(aider)" (2024-07 to 2025-07) or prefixed "aider:" (2023-05 to 2024-06, the GPT-4 era). For sampled (commit, file) pairs, git blame at the last commit before +14 days counts surviving lines. Results are stratified by change size (strata with fewer than 15 pairs per arm are dropped). churn_mult = churn_AI / churn_human − 1, with 800 bootstraps.

Placement. Measured churn alone lowers P(CONFIRM) from 88% to 65%. The factors that decide the verdict are not in git:

No second open repo with AI attribution was found. kaiaulu has none, and this repo has 90 Claude-co-authored commits but only 8 days of history. That scarcity is a finding in itself.

C3.3 archpat: Helix

Method. 674 production .java files in the pattern4-scanned modules at the 2026-05-24 snapshot (149 patterned, from Part A's pattern4 output). History is mapped through renames. Per file: bytes, commits, bug-fix commits (subject regex), SZZ bug-introductions (Part A's szz_pairs.csv). Negative-binomial GLMs give rate ratios, patterned vs not, controlling for log size and log churn, with 300 bootstraps. Patterned is compared with non-patterned, not with Legacy, because Legacy is defined by bug count, so comparing against it would be circular.
