Testing SE laws: what Brooks, aiwork and archpat taught us, and a protocol for the next law

Status: 2026-10-28. Reproduce with make laws evidence sensitivity (evidence needs clones of apache/helix and Aider-AI/aider). Per-law detail lives in the evidence cards next to this file: brooks (the standard Brooks model), aiwork, archpat, archpat_cost, sensitivity.

TL;DR

  1. None of the three laws is true or false on its own terms. Each is a capacity (Cartwright) that shows up only when particular support factors are present. The useful output is the map of where it holds, plus a test of whether a given project sits inside it.
  2. All three turn on the price of pulling the lever: coordination for Brooks, verification for aiwork, migration for archpat. In every case the original model either fixed that price or left it out, and git history records it worst.
  3. Real data mostly overturned the defaults.
  4. Helix's onboarding drag is about 1/24 of the Brooks default, and its coordination optimum is about 5.4 veterans. So doubling Helix's typical 3-person core helps; doubling it at its peak size (6–8) hurts.
  5. Aider's AI-written lines churn about 1.33× as much as the same author's own lines, stable across two model generations. The default assumes 3×.
  6. Helix's patterned files are only modestly safer than other files (0.68–0.83×, against an assumed 0.25×) and no more productive (1.05×, against an assumed 2.5×).
  7. Sample size bought nothing. 100k Monte Carlo runs at ±15% give "100% CONFIRMED". The same Brooks claim sampled over its declared ranges holds in about 10% of worlds. Robustness depends on how big the neighbourhood is, not on how many runs.
  8. The structural battery is necessary but was too weak. The existing 8-test bank passes archpat on every test, yet archpat has a dead parameter, a hard-coded benefit channel and free migration. Tests must state their expectation first and check the declared mechanism, or they can't fail.

3. Results per law

Brooks (the standard model)

Since 2026-11-06 lawkit keeps one Brooks model for every test and report: - a late project (backlog 1000), scored by finish time; - sd.brooks' communication and training costs, plus five literature structures as uncertain settings (newcomer output, attrition, unsplittable work, overtime, rework), each off at the default; - bounded overload (time left for work = exp(-overhead)); - dt 0.125.

The four earlier Brooks models are archived in outputs/archive/2026-11-06_before_standard_brooks/.

  • Machine: all 10 structure tests pass. The time-step error is 1%. The knock-out passes: with talking and training neutralised, hiring helps, +2. Every data-free Forrester test passes (outputs/forrester_all.md).
  • Robustness: ±15%: 100% CONFIRM. Prior: 44 / 16 / 40 (CONFIRM / REFUTE / neutral). Uniform: 27 / 33 / 40.
  • Default effect: 10 hires delay the finish by 11 ticks (25 → 36). The earlier straight-line model said 88, because its overloaded teams froze at zero output.
  • Decided by team size and talking cost (importance Vet 0.66, comm_coef 0.25, leave 0.09). The coordination optimum is V*: V(2V − 1) = 2/c, i.e. V* ≈ 14 for c=0.005 and ≈ 5.4 for Helix's c≈0.038. The law holds when the post-hire team overshoots V*. It fails for small cores (small_core: REFUTE, +51). Tipping point on team size: 7.5.
  • Timing barely matters once the project is truly late: early, mid and last-minute hires all CONFIRM (−12, −11, −9).
  • Variants: squads (−5), async (−3) and per-person communication (−6) shrink the delay. Mentor pods and shared mentoring remove it at the default.
  • Helix (152 months; re-fitted with the same bounded formula): c = 0.038 [0.021, 0.066], train = 0.008 [0, 0.037], and newcomers who stay are productive almost at once.
  • Doubling the median core of 3 → REFUTE 70% (hiring helps).
  • Doubling 6 or 8 → CONFIRM 87–94%.
  • Doubling 12 is outside Helix's observed range (max 8): extrapolation.
  • Policy sensitivity within Helix's measured values: outputs/forrester_all.md §2.
  • Aider (one maintainer plus drive-bys): REFUTE 87%. The support factors for Brooks are absent.

aiwork (does full AI use lower kept code?)

  • Machine: knock-out passes (removing churn and verify drag makes AI help, +38). The FAIL is a real structural defect: churn + mature outflow can exceed the Wip stock in one tick (churn_base up to 1, churn_mult up to 5), so 24% of CONFIRMs exist only because a negative stock is clamped to 0. Bound the outflows.
  • Robustness: ±15%: 100%. Prior: 88%. Uniform: 74%.
  • Decided by speed vs verification, not churn. Importance: gen_boost 0.42, verify_drag 0.40, churn_mult 0.07. Approximate steady-state condition for AI to help: (1 + gen_boost)(1 − verify_drag) > 1 + churn_mult · churn_base / (mature_rate + churn_base). The defaults give 0.78 < 1.40, so AI hurts mainly because default AI is slower (1.3 × 0.6), with churn secondary.
  • Scenarios split cleanly: vibe_coding, boilerplate and copilot_rct REFUTE; review_gate, legacy_expert, half_adoption and short_sprint CONFIRM. METR, GitClear and Peng sample different regions, which agrees with the repo's existing grid_aiwork reading.
  • Aider evidence (same author, same period, size-stratified 14-day blame survival): AI 49.7% vs human 37.2% churn → churn_mult = 0.35 [0.18, 0.54]. The GPT-4 era (2023-05..2024-06) gives 0.32 [0.16, 0.52]. The churn premium is real, about 6× smaller than the default, and stable across model generations.
  • Placement: measured churn alone → CONFIRM 65%. With METR-like speed/verify factors borrowed → 100%; with Peng-like → 3% (REFUTE 85%). What to measure next: verify_drag (gain 0.34), then gen_boost (0.15). Neither is visible in git.

archpat (does aggressive migration to patterns repair a bad project?)

  • Machine, reference model: the dead parameter pat_strength FAILs. The knock-out FAILs: with patterned code given the same productivity and bug rate as legacy, migration still helps (+27), via the hard-coded pay × (1 + 0.5·share). Migration is free: available is computed and never used.
  • Robustness (reference): ±15%: 100%. Prior: 65%. Uniform: 59%. Decided by gen_leg / gen_pat and born_*.
  • Mechanism variants reverse it. Give migration a price (4 items per module) and prior CONFIRM falls from 66% to 20% (REFUTE 59%). Add refactoring-induced bugs (Bavota 2012) and pressure-driven erosion and it becomes 4% CONFIRM / 88% REFUTE.
  • Helix evidence (674 production files, 149 patterned; NB-GLM controlling size and churn): fix-commit rate ratio 0.83 [0.71, 0.93], SZZ bug-introduction ratio 0.68 [0.54, 0.86] (model assumes 0.25), productivity proxy 1.05 [0.91, 1.27] (assumes 2.5).
  • Placement: with free migration, Helix CONFIRMs (82%). With an uncertain price it REFUTEs (94%). The measured ratios barely move either number. Break-even (Helix stocks): migrating a module must cost less than about 1 feature-item of effort with no injected bugs, or less than about 0.1 if each migration injects 0.25 debt. That price is the measurement that decides the law, and it can be measured: RefactoringMiner + SZZ, both kaiaulu-wrapped.

4. A conclusive strategy for testing SE laws (goal 1)

What each test family can establish, judged by what actually changed a conclusion in this study:

test family establishes cannot establish changed a conclusion here?
Forrester/Sterman structure + metamorphic the model is a coherent machine (no dead knobs, no hidden channels, no clamp-made verdicts) that the law is true anywhere Yes: found 7 model defects (B3, B7, B8, archpat ×3, aiwork overshoot)
Monte Carlo at ±15% precision of the default verdict robustness No: always "100%"
Sampling over declared ranges how much the claim depends on the author's priors where real projects are Yes: 100% → 6–11% (Brooks)
Regime map (tipping + rules) which support factors decide, and thresholds (V*, the aiwork inequality, archpat break-even) which side a real project is on Yes: turned verdicts into conditions
Business scenarios consequences in named worlds whether those worlds exist Partly: exposed B7 (not late)
Mechanism variants whether the verdict survives rival structures which structure is right Yes: archpat reverses
Real-project placement P(law holds here | measured support factors) the law's average effect Yes: Helix flips Brooks and archpat
Value of information what to measure next — Yes: always pointed away from what git records

Recommended order (cheap → expensive; stop when a step fails):

  1. Claim hygiene. State the lever, arms, outcome in the law's own words, the antecedent (e.g. "late"), the support factors and the causal channels. Stop if the default world violates the antecedent.
  2. Machine battery. Stop on a dead parameter, a failed knock-out, a dt-flip, or clamp-made CONFIRMs above 20%.
  3. Three-neighbourhood robustness. Never report one number.
  4. Regime map, then derive the dimensionless threshold analytically where possible.
  5. Rival mechanisms for the term that prices the lever.
  6. Placement on at least two populations that differ in the deciding factor, with a written caveat chain per estimate.
  7. VOI → next measurement, phrased as a tool + column (kaiaulu function where one exists).

Verdict format. "L plays a causal role in Y in populations where S holds; Helix satisfies S with probability p; the deciding unknown is F." Not "the law is CONFIRMED".

5. Reading the results through Cartwright

  • It worked there vs. it will work here (Cartwright & Hardie 2012). METR, Peng and GitClear each show "it worked there". Section 6 of each card asks the policy question for a named project. The two answers diverge: Brooks holds in the textbook world and fails for Helix's typical core.
  • Laws as capacities in nomological machines (Cartwright 1983, 1989, 1999). A law shows up only inside a shielded arrangement of support factors. The regime map is the machine description: for Brooks, a late project + post-hire team > V*(c) + training drag that's large relative to pre-deadline output. Outside it, "adding people" is just adding capacity.
  • The causal cake / INUS (Mackie 1965). The lever is one slice. rules() and tipping() find the other slices. The knock-out test checks that the slices the model declares are the ones doing the work; archpat failed this, because a hidden slice baked the conclusion in.
  • Vertical and horizontal search. Vertical: re-describe the lever more abstractly until it travels ("adding people" → "adding coordination load past the optimum"; "using AI" → "trading verification time for generation speed"; "migrating to patterns" → "paying a migration price for lower future defect rates"). Horizontal: list the support factors that must be present (variants + scenarios).
  • Evidence for use is a long chain. Data → construct → parameter → model → verdict. Every mined number carries its caveat list (commits ≠ productivity; within-author ≠ randomised; cross-sectional pattern associations ≠ the effect of migrating). Sensitivity tables show which link holds the weight. For archpat it's the unmeasured price, not the measured ratios.
  • The streetlight. VOI always ranked the factor git can't see (verification time, migration price, lateness) above the one it can (churn, file bug rates). That points the measurement agenda, rather than collecting more of what is easy.
  • Borrowing support factors needs a similarity argument. Plugging METR's or Peng's speed/verify numbers into Aider swings the verdict from 100% to 3%. Which population Aider resembles is an empirical claim that has to be argued, not assumed.
  • Gold standards don't travel by themselves. The within-author Aider design is strong on internal validity (same person, same months, same files) and still says nothing about teams with real code review. Rigour at the study site does not license export.

6. A generic framework for future SE laws (goal 2)

Implemented as lawkit/ (see its README). A new law is one Law(...) spec plus, optionally, one miner that writes outputs/evidence/<law>_<project>.json. On the untouched sd.debt it immediately reports a hard-coded channel (knock-out FAIL) and a claim no background in its ranges can refute (100% CONFIRM). Those are the same failure types found in archpat.

Reusable knowledge each run extracts:

  1. A defect catalogue for models: dead parameters, hard-coded channels, clamp-made verdicts, outflow overshoot, outcome ≠ the law's words, antecedent false by default. These recur across the 35 sd.py models and can be checked automatically.
  2. Regime thresholds in dimensionless form (V*, the aiwork inequality, the archpat break-even price). These travel between projects where raw parameters don't.
  3. A measurement agenda (VOI), mapped to tools and columns.
  4. A cross-law heuristic: price the lever first. Every "doing L improves/hurts Y" law is a claim about the sign of (benefit − price of L). Before building anything, ask (a) what pulling L costs, (b) is that cost in the model, and (c) is it in any data source. Missing (b) gives a tautology (archpat); missing (c) gives an undecidable verdict (aiwork).
  5. A composition path. resources.txt sketches chaining Brooks → aiwork → archpat. lawkit composes laws as one Law over a combined model whose factors are the union of the parts' factors. The battery then checks the joint machine, and VOI ranks unknowns across the chain. Not built yet; the specs are designed so it can be.

7. Limitations and next steps

  • Python, not .Rmd + .R. The miners use plain git, not kaiaulu (no R on this machine). Porting mine/ to kaiaulu notebooks per Carlos's format is mechanical but not done. Identity merging (identity_match) isn't applied.
  • Two projects, one ecosystem each. Brooks and archpat have Helix only (Ambari has pattern4 output too); aiwork has Aider only. No other open repo with AI attribution turned up (kaiaulu has none). That scarcity is itself a finding.
  • Priors I chose. The archpat_cost price priors (mode 2 items/module, 0.25 debt/module) and the aiwork scenario values are my judgement. The break-even table exists so readers can replace them.
  • Next measurements, by VOI:
  • archpat mig_cost / mig_bugs: RefactoringMiner (parse_java_code_refactoring_json) + SZZ, in the style of Bavota et al. 2012, on Helix and Ambari.
  • aiwork verify_drag: review latency and review-comment counts on AI-tagged vs. untagged PRs (GitHub API).
  • Brooks lateness: JIRA fixVersion due dates (parse_jira), so "late" is observed rather than assumed.
  • Model fixes to upstream to paper/sd.py: use completion time for brooks; bound the aiwork outflows; for archpat, use available and price migration, remove or wire pat_strength, and make the 0.5 paydown a declared parameter.
